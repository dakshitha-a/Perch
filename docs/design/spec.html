Perch

See every route to fund your work.

Perch reads a Temple researcher's public papers, or a student's CV, and lays out the routes to fund that work: open federal grants and industry leads for a researcher, fellowships and scholarships for a student. Every claim is cited to its record, the hit rate is measured, and it runs on any model, including one inside Temple.

Technical specification v2.0 (as built, S33, 2 Nov 2026) · Temple AI Build Challenge, Fall 2026 · owner: Dakshitha (Temple University) · Public data only. This version describes the product as it is built on main, not as it was planned. Items marked verify are believed true and have not been checked against the source; UNVERIFIED marks a number or claim the repository does not yet hold. The Markdown copy for the coding agent is docs/SPEC.md; this page is rebuilt from docs/design/spec.html.

Users, working backwards

The question nobody at Temple can answer quickly today: if this lab’s federal line ends or is cut, what else fits this specific body of work, and what is the evidence? Pivot-RP and SPIN answer “which calls match these keywords?”. Perch answers the question above, from the researcher’s own papers, with a measured hit rate.

A student has the same shape of question: which fellowships and scholarships could I apply for, what do I still need, and what would I say for myself? Perch answers it from the student’s CV, checked by rule against each record, with the points an application could make. Both audiences are primary users and are sold equally (Dakshitha, 2026-11-01).

1 · The principal investigator primary

A Temple faculty member whose public federal award ends within 18 months, or was stopped. Has 20 minutes, not a day.

Job: see the realistic alternatives for my work, ranked, with reasons I can check, then decide which office to talk to.

Reads: the brief. Does: keeps or dismisses leads, asks Scout follow-ups, edits and sends the hand-off. May add a CV, a draft proposal, a research direction or unpublished work to the session.

2 · The student (undergraduate or graduate) primary

A Temple student with a CV and a few achievements, looking for fellowships and scholarships they can actually apply for.

Job: see the options I may be eligible for, each checked against my CV by rule, with what I still need; the points my application could make; a note to the right office, which I send myself.

Reads: the student brief. Does: keeps options, compares them, asks Scout, edits and sends the note. The session is private by construction: choosing Student opens it before any text leaves the browser.

3 · Research Development and Technology Commercialization receive researcher hand-offs

Research Development (Office of the Vice President for Research) receives grant-route hand-offs that already contain the evidence. Technology Commercialization receives industry-route hand-offs.

Industry leads are labelled as inferences from public signals. The app never says “should partner”; it says “these public signals suggest interest; not verified.” Nothing in-app in v1; the hand-off is a draft the researcher sends.

4 · Scholar Development and Fellowships Advising; Student Financial Services receive student notes

Scholar Development and Fellowships Advising receives a student’s note about public fellowships, with the rule read-out and what is still needed. Student Financial Services receives a student’s note about scholarships.

Until a real feed is licensed, the scholarship records are a SYNTHETIC sample, and the note says so.

Out of scope for v1

Postdocs (NIH F32 and F33 listings are shown to a student only to say why they do not fit); department-wide batch scans (opt-in, v2); anything built from real unpublished or confidential text (every test and demo of documents uses SYNTHETIC text). The scholarship corpus is a SYNTHETIC sample until Temple’s own feed is licensed: ScholarshipUniverse, which Temple runs for its students, is the natural source under its contract verify: no public API, export format or field list was found; terms unverified. The fellowship list is public.

Why this is not another Pivot

Temple subscribes to Pivot-RP, SPIN and Dimensions. Every slide has to survive the question “so what does yours do that those don’t?”. The honest comparison, using those tools as Temple’s own pages describe them verify current features before the slide; quote their descriptions, never ours:

The one-line answer for the judges: Pivot finds things that mention your keywords. Perch reads what you have published, or what your CV says, tells you which routes fit and why, shows you how often it is right, and plugs into whatever AI platform Temple already uses.

Exactly what goes in and what comes out

In · researcher

  • Researcher identity, one of: name + “Temple University”; an ORCID iD; an OpenAlex author ID; or up to 20 DOIs. Resolved to one OpenAlex author record; the user confirms the match before anything runs. An exact id with one match opens Ready to build at once.
  • Tell Perch more (optional): a research direction and include keywords, which steer the search; exclude keywords, which filter; a career stage, which keeps career-stage calls with a “You said” note.
  • Documents (optional, held in the session only): a CV, a draft proposal, a research direction, unpublished work; pasted or uploaded as text, Markdown, DOCX or PDF. The draft proposal is the strongest steer (0.5 of the document weight), the direction 0.3, unpublished work 0.2; documents take 0.6 of the thread weight beside the public papers. The CV is read by rule for career-stage eligibility and prior sponsors. A documents-only brief needs no public identity. Tests and demos use SYNTHETIC text.

In · student

  • A CV, pasted or uploaded (text, Markdown, DOCX or PDF). Code reads level, year, major, GPA, enrolment, expected graduation and the number of referees by rule.
  • An interests note, which steers the search.
  • Up to five achievements, each a session record an explanation or a cover point may cite.
  • No public identity. The session is private by construction: choosing Student opens it before any text leaves the browser.

Out · the researcher’s brief

  • Research profile: research threads from the papers, topic labels and a short summary tied to work IDs, key papers (title, year, venue, DOI link), public federal awards with start and end dates, and the public award horizon: the end date of the last public award. Nothing about lab finances is inferred.
  • Grants: the 10 best open Grants.gov opportunities after eligibility rules, each with sponsor, title, deadline words, award size and eligibility copied from the record, a Match score on a 0–100 scale computed by code, and a three-sentence fit explanation that cites the researcher’s own papers. Weak matches fold under the stronger ones.
  • Industry leads: companies with at least two kinds of public signal (company works citing the researcher; NIH SBIR/STTR awards from RePORTER in the topic; company works in the topic), the signals listed, no model-written text. Every lead carries “inferred from public signals; not verified”. Papers with more than 30 authors do not seed this route.
  • Compare: the kept leads side by side; every cell is copied from or counted from a record. No effort, IP or likelihood column, because no record states them.
  • Hand-off draft: the model writes a 2–4 sentence opening; code writes the rest from the kept leads; office names come from research.temple.edu; the To field is left empty; copy or open in mail. Under the To line, the screen and the researcher export carry a fixed note: before anything goes to a sponsor, Temple routes it through ERA for institutional review and approval, with a link to Find your research administrator. Never sent or stored by the app.
  • Application points (skill 4, S33, on request through Ask Scout): for one record, 3 to 6 points a cover letter could make. Each point pairs what the record asks for or rewards, cited to the record, with what the applicant has, cited to the CV, a document, a paper or a public award; up to three things the record asks for that nothing added shows are listed too. Checked by the validator and the grounding model; shown on the card with Copy; never in exports or hand-off drafts.
  • Evidence rail: every claim links to the record it came from. A claim with no record is not shown.
  • Export: Markdown, a print page the browser saves as PDF, and JSON of the records with provenance. The full brief is printed, with a note when it includes text drawn from documents. An optional box masks phrases from ongoing research. Each lead also prints the record facts the card shows: Match, status, rule notes, higher-education applicants and an excerpt of the description.

Out · the student brief

  • Your options: two lists, Fellowships (public) from Temple’s Scholar Development and Fellowships Advising list and NIH F-series listings, and Scholarships and awards (sample) from the SYNTHETIC corpus, never in one list. Each option carries a verdict by rule (eligible, may be eligible, not eligible), the rule it rests on, Still needed (what the record asks for that the CV does not show), and a one-sentence fit explanation checked against the listing and the student’s own text. Deadline and amount copied from the record. Public fellowship listings get “may be eligible” at most, because their own pages set the full rules.
  • What Perch read: the rule read-out of the CV, so the student can check it and correct it by replacing the CV.
  • Application points (the same skill 4): for one fellowship or sample scholarship, from the CV, the interests note and the achievements. A sample scholarship gives sample points, and the card says so.
  • Compare and the note: kept options in two tables, public and sample apart; a code-written note to Scholar Development and Fellowships Advising or Student Financial Services. Never sent by the app.
  • Export with a sample banner; the full brief, including the CV values in verdict lines, with a note that it includes text from the student's documents.

Private by default: sessions, documents, Local or Cloud

Perch has no user accounts, so since S23 (2026-10-29, Dakshitha) it keeps nothing about the people who use it. Every brief, with or without documents, is a session held in the server’s memory. Models run on the server unless a user turns Cloud on. Standing rule 1 was amended on 2026-11-01 (Q33) so that a user may send their own documents to the cloud provider when they choose to, and rule 5 on the same day so that a session’s models may read the CV with contact details blanked. Design: docs/design/PRIVATE_MODE.md; user guide: docs/private-mode.md.

What a session is

  • Nothing stored about you: no briefs, leads, keep and dismiss choices, drafts, Scout conversations or per-user logs. The browser keeps only the theme and, in the tab, the session id so a reload resumes the brief.
  • Public records stay cached: OpenAlex researchers and works, RePORTER and NSF awards and the companies behind industry leads are written to the store, so the next brief is faster. They hold nothing about the person who asked.
  • Ends with End session, after 30 minutes idle (10 minutes if never used), or when the server restarts.
  • One side at a time (2026-11-02, Dakshitha): a session holds a researcher brief or a student brief, never both. Opening the other side’s form shows a warning that lists what the session holds; ending it starts the form fresh, and the server refuses the other side’s brief until then.
  • Warm demo briefs: the demo researchers’ default briefs are built when the server starts and held in memory; a visitor gets a copy in their own session at once, and the card says so.
  • Isolated: a random session token, memory and size caps, a rate limit of its own. The store is an overlay on a read-only base; no cache, log or index writes from a session; core dumps off.

Local or Cloud, and the documents consent

  • Local is the default. Every model runs on the Perch server, which the privacy statement names by operator.
  • Cloud is a choice. Where the deployer sets PERCH_CLOUD, the chip at the top opens a dialog. Before it turns on, a warning names the provider and says what is sent: what you type and the text of the public papers and listings in your brief. The user confirms first; the API checks that the confirm names the provider. Only the four model skills move to the cloud model, and their text is tagged with the provider; search, ranking, the checks and Scout’s own model stay local.
  • Documents go only after a second warning (rule 1 as amended; PERCH_CLOUD_DOCUMENTS=1, set on the demo). It names the provider and lists each document in the session as it was added, says that a CV carries contact details and career facts and that a draft or manuscript is unpublished research, that the provider’s API terms then apply and Perch stores nothing, and that the choice lasts for this session and can be turned off. Nothing is sent before a ticked box and a confirm. Students get the same warning with the CV wording.
  • Consent is per document and per session: held as a hash of kind and text in session memory only; a document added or replaced later turns Cloud off until it is confirmed too; turning Cloud off or ending the session clears it.
  • The deployer’s choice: fully local (no key, nothing leaves the server), hybrid (local by default, Cloud by the user’s choice; how the demo runs) or cloud-only; and, with a hosted provider, whether the API key is a zero-data-retention key, if the provider offers one. The privacy statement says which. A zero-data-retention key is a deployment setting, not a feature Perch builds (Dakshitha, 2026-11-01).

Documents and the models

  • Code reads the CV by rule for career-stage eligibility (NIH’s early-stage definition, as “may be eligible per your CV”), prior sponsors, and the student verdicts.
  • Models may read the CV and documents (rule 5 as amended, 2026-11-01). Contact details (emails, phone numbers, links, ORCID) are blanked in every model prompt view, for every skill. The CV is capped at about 3,500 characters in a prompt, preferring education, appointments, funding and awards. Skill 4, application points, is the one skill that reads the CV text as such, and only for the one record the user asks about.
  • Citations: a document is a session-only record (private: id, no URL) the validator accepts inside the session; a citation to it shows a lock. Numbers and dates still come from a cited record.
  • Leaving the app: while a session holds documents, every model-written text counts as drawn from them: the hand-off leaves it out and opens with a plain sentence, and opt-in masking there replaces identifiers and novel phrases with labels such as [PERSON-1], unmaskable on screen. Exports (changed 2 Nov 2026) give the full brief with a note that it includes text from the user's documents and stays on their computer; their one mask, "Mask my ongoing research", replaces phrases from a draft proposal, research direction or unpublished work with [PRIVATE-PHRASE-n]. The warning says masking cannot hide the research direction. Application points never enter an export or a hand-off.
  • Checked, not promised: every SYNTHETIC test document carries a canary token; after a full session no token may appear in any file, log, temp file, browser storage or outbound request (tests/test_private.py). The live canary (scripts/private_canary.py) was clean on the rebuilt demo in S32, including the six model-server journals, and after every QA round.

Walkthrough: researcher and student, side by side

Researcher · five screens plus Ask Scout

Find — choose Researcher; type a name, ORCID or OpenAlex id; pick the right author from the candidates. Open Tell Perch more for a direction, keywords, a career stage, a draft proposal or a CV, or fill it with a SYNTHETIC sample. Press Build brief.
Routes — the brief opens here. Grants and Industry on a pill; each lead a card with its Match, the record’s own fields, the cited explanation, Keep and Dismiss. Kept leads collect in the shortlist; the evidence rail opens from any title. Weak matches fold.

Compare — the kept leads side by side, every cell from a record.

Ask Scout — plain follow-ups on the brief: “Which of my NSF leads close by 1 Mar 2027?”, “Why does the top lead fit?”, “What could my cover letter for the top lead say?” (runs cover_points and puts the checked points on the card with Copy).

Student · five steps

Student — choose Student on Find; the session opens before any text is sent. Paste or upload the CV; add an interests note and up to five achievements; Build brief.
Your options — two lists, Fellowships (public) and Scholarships and awards (sample), each option with its verdict by rule, Still needed and a checked fit sentence; Keep the ones worth a closer look. Sort by Match or by closing date.
What Perch read — the rule read-out of the CV, to check, and correct by replacing the CV.
Compare — kept options in two tables, public and sample apart; pick Scholar Development and Fellowships Advising or Student Financial Services, and a code-written note appears to edit, copy or open in mail.
Ask Scout — “Which of these close first?” or “What should my application for this scholarship highlight?”: the second runs cover_points on the CV, the interests note and the achievements. On a sample scholarship, the card and the answer say the points are for an invented sample.

Demo Day: the demo researchers’ briefs open at once because they were built at server start; a live brief takes about 40 s on the demo models. The rehearsal script runs three researcher questions, the student question and the documents hand-off in fresh sessions: 45 of 45 on the demo (S32). The student path runs on a SYNTHETIC persona.

Architecture

Solid outlines are deterministic code; the tinted box is the only place a language model runs, and the agent inside it can call nothing but the deterministic functions on the left. Dashed boxes are outside the system. Every arrow into the store carries provenance; every arrow out of the model box passes the citation validator (code), and the grounding check (Clef) flags unsupported sentences. The model box may also read a session's CV and documents, with contact details blanked, and retains nothing. Students run the same pipeline on a CV, with verdicts by rule.

Scout, the agent, and the platforms it runs on

Two layers, deliberately separated. The deterministic pipeline builds a brief the same way every time; it is what the back-test measures. Scout sits on top of it: the model turns a question into calls on Perch’s own tools, then writes a short answer that cites the records those tools returned. Everything else is code. Specified from Perch’s own needs in docs/design/SCOUT_SPEC.md (approved 2026-10-30, Q37) and built as phase 1 in S28.

What Scout does (S28)

  • Each step runs as soon as the model asks for it. A Stop button ends the question between steps; the steps already done stay on screen. There is no ask-before-every-step mode: it was removed in S28, because Scout’s tools only read records and write text, so a gate cost a click and bought nothing.
  • Above the answer, “How Scout read your question” lists each step with its filters as chips, for example “Searched opportunities: activity code R21, closes by 2027-03-01”. This is the control for a misread question. The step cards fold under it, each saying where it ran: code, a local model, or the named Cloud provider.
  • Follow-ups in plain language: filters the page does not offer (an activity code, a ceiling, a close-by date, words to include or leave out), “why” questions built from records, and chores across steps such as compare then draft.
  • The answer is two to five short sentences; each citation opens the record in the evidence rail. A failed answer is rewritten once, then withheld and the page says so.
  • Fixed subject: Scout works on the brief’s own researcher or student. The web app’s researcher tools take no author id and have no author search; the MCP server keeps both.

What Scout cannot do

  • Call anything outside its tool allowlist: no web browsing, no free-text fetch, no shell.
  • State a number or date that is not in a cited record or the person’s own question (the validator applies to the agent exactly as to the pipeline; a user-typed number is kept only outside a cited sentence).
  • Send email, change a record, or write to any outside system.
  • Run past its budgets: 8 model calls per question; 120,000 tokens and $0.25 per session, counting the fit, hand-off and cover-points steps; 30 questions an hour per address (300 on Demo Day). History compaction keeps every call under about 6,400 tokens; “Start a new conversation” resets it.
  • Read anything about a researcher that is not a public record or a document they added to the session.

Researcher tools (ten)

resolve_author · build_profile · list_awards · search_opportunities · get_org_signals · explain_fit · compare · draft_handoff · cover_points · get_record

Each is a thin wrapper over the pipeline. resolve_author is on the MCP server only; in the web app the other nine are fixed to the brief’s researcher. explain_fit, draft_handoff and cover_points call a model and follow the session’s Local or Cloud choice; the rest call none. Every call is logged with argument and result hashes in an operator run; a visitor’s session writes no Scout log. A repeated identical call in one question is answered from the earlier result.

Student tools (eight)

search_options · check_option · explain_verdict · read_cv · compare_options · draft_note · cover_points · get_record

All but cover_points return rule results, record fields and the fields code read from the CV, and call no model. Student Scout runs only in a private session and is not on the MCP server. Sample scholarships are marked as samples in every tool result, and an answer may not cite a sample and a public fellowship in one list or sentence.

Skill 4: application points (cover_points, S33)

For one record (a grant for a researcher; a public fellowship or a sample scholarship for a student), the skill returns 3 to 6 points a cover letter could make. Each point has two halves: an “asks” half, what the record asks for or rewards, cited to the record; and a “you” half, what the applicant has that answers it, cited to the CV, a document, a paper or a public award (for a student: the CV, the interests note or an achievement). It may list up to three gaps: things the record asks for that nothing the user added shows. Every point passes the citation check (ids known; numbers and dates only from the cited record), then the grounding model flags any unsupported sentence; a failed set is regenerated once, then withheld. Code writes the answer line; the full points sit on the Scout card with a Copy button, as a hand-off draft does, for the person to put in their own words. They never enter an export or a hand-off draft. It runs on the session’s hand-off role: local unless Cloud is chosen and the documents warning confirmed. It is the one tool whose model reads the CV text, with contact details blanked and the CV capped at about 3,500 characters, and only for the one record asked about; a researcher with no CV gets points from their papers and public awards. On a sample scholarship the card and the answer say the points are for an invented sample. Through MCP it works from papers and public awards, because documents cannot be added through MCP. Its bench numbers (two tasks on the local model) are UNVERIFIED until S33 task 5 runs.

Any model, any platform

Provider layer

One interface for chat, tool calling and structured output. Adapters: Anthropic API; any OpenAI-compatible endpoint; Ollama for local models. Model per role is set in .env: profile summary, fit explainer, hand-off (which application points share), Scout and decide. Embeddings, reranking and the grounding check are always local.

Small local models call tools less reliably, so Scout has a structured policy: one JSON step per turn, plus the validator. Native tool calling stays first (qwen3:8b: 17 of 20 native, 12 of 20 structured).

MCP server (S14)

perch mcp serves the ten researcher tools over stdio to any MCP client (MCP Python SDK 2.3.0), with the same argument checks and validator; every result carries a provenance map of the records to cite, and the client’s own model writes the answer. Budgets: 60 tool calls and 20 model-backed calls per connection. --private runs with no writes or logs and refuses to start unless every model is self-hosted. Documents cannot be added through MCP. Tested live from the SDK client and from Claude Code; a local Ollama agent was not tested (UNVERIFIED); Copilot verify.

Monitoring (S16, shelved S23)

A weekly watch was built: refresh open listings as a delta, run a fresh brief per watched researcher, compare it with the last run, and draft a notification by code when a public award ends within 12 months or a new opportunity enters the top 10. Live once on the demo (660 listings, 3 new, 0 triggers). It needs to know whom to watch, and Perch keeps nothing about its users, so it lives in perch/shelved/monitor/ with a README on reconnecting it once a user system exists.

Rule enforced in code, for pipeline and agent alike: a model output that cites an id not present in its input, or that contains a dollar amount or date not present in the cited records, is rejected and regenerated once; if it fails again the lead is shown without an explanation and flagged, or the answer is withheld. The pass rate is reported per model.

Explanation shape, as built. Each fit explanation is three sentences: a factual sentence about the opportunity and a factual sentence about the research, both checked by the grounder against their cited records, plus a link sentence shown as an inference and checked by layer 1 only. The grounder scores fit judgments (“aligns with”) low even when both facts are in the records, so the judgment is labelled rather than pretended to be verified. Fit text reads in the second person on the card and in exports.

Responsible design, as the judges phrase it

Risks considered

  • Wrong or unsupported claims → the citation validator and the grounding check on every model output; numbers and dates only from records; a failed output withheld and flagged.
  • Reputational: naming people and companies → demo researchers appear by alias in the docs; personas are SYNTHETIC; industry leads carry an inference label and the hand-off names no company; masking hides identifiers in hand-offs and ongoing-research phrases in exports.
  • Over-reliance → the brief is labelled a starting point; the Match is a text match, not the odds of funding; hand-offs and notes go to a human office and the user sends them.
  • A misread question → “How Scout read your question” shows every filter Scout applied.

Protected data

  • Public records only in the store; nothing stored about a user; every brief a session in memory.
  • Documents stay on local models unless the user chooses Cloud and confirms, per document, which ones leave; contact details blanked before any model sees a CV; nothing retained.
  • Proved by canary tests, not promised: no trace in files, logs, temp files, browser storage or outbound requests.
  • Department-wide scans are opt-in and v2; monitoring stays shelved until accounts exist.

People in control

  • Author match confirmed by the user before any run; a student’s CV read-out shown to check.
  • Keep / dismiss on every lead; Stop on every Scout question; nothing is sent by the app.
  • Research Development, Technology Commercialization, Scholar Development and Fellowships Advising and Student Financial Services decide; the tool proposes.
  • The deployer decides where models run and what key they use.

Accessibility and feasibility

  • Keyboard-navigable, labelled controls, contrast checked, light and dark, works on a phone from a QR code; the full walk with axe-core shows 0 violations on 217 states; the checklist is docs/design/A11Y_CHECKLIST.md (12 of 13 at S25; zoom not rerun).
  • Runs on one node with one command; cost per brief measured, not guessed; every AI tool and model logged in docs/AI_USE_LOG.md.
